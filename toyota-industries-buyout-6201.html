Elliott Pushed a ¥16,300 Buyout to ¥20,600. Then the Company Delisted.

Toyota Industries Corporation · TYO:6201 · Activist: Elliott Investment Management

Toyota Motor, group real-estate arm Toyota Fudosan, and Chairman Akio Toyoda announced a tender offer for Toyota Industries at ¥16,300 per share on 2025-06-03. Under sustained public pressure from Elliott Investment Management, the price rose to ¥18,800 (2026-01-14) and then ¥20,600 (2026-03-02) — a 26.4% increase, with no competing bidder. The tender offer closed successfully at roughly ¥5.9 trillion, the largest M&A transaction between Japanese companies on record, and Toyota Industries delisted on 2026-06-01.

Three numbers, nine months

Toyota Motor, Toyota Fudosan, and Chairman Akio Toyoda announced a tender offer for Toyota Industries at ¥16,300 per share on June 3, 2025. By January 14, 2026, the price was ¥18,800. By March 2, 2026, it was ¥20,600, with the tender period extended to March 23. ¥16,300 to ¥20,600 is a 26.4% increase, achieved without a competing bidder — just sustained pressure from a shareholder who said the first number was wrong.

Who pushed

Elliott Investment Management said so directly, and said it in public. The fund published a formal statement opposing "Toyota Fudosan's Revised Tender Offer for Toyota Industries Corporation" while the price was still at ¥18,800, arguing the number remained inadequate. The final revision, three weeks later, took the price past what Elliott had been pushing for. Elliott ultimately tendered its shares once the price reached ¥20,600, calling the final number an improved outcome for minority shareholders.

The close

The tender offer closed successfully on March 23, 2026, at roughly ¥5.9 trillion — the largest M&A transaction between Japanese companies on record. An extraordinary shareholders meeting on May 12, at the company's Kariya, Aichi headquarters, approved the share consolidation used to squeeze out remaining minority holders. Toyota Industries delisted from the Tokyo Stock Exchange Prime Market and the Nagoya Stock Exchange Premier Market on June 1, 2026. Cash from the squeeze-out is expected to be distributed to remaining shareholders around late August 2026.

Why this belongs next to the other kind of result

Quorum has spent several issues documenting a ceiling: activist proposals asking Japanese boards to change course — dismiss a president, sell a cross-holding, remove a chairman — running into a domestic shareholder base that doesn't move past roughly 25–30%, even with proxy-advisor backing. See the 2026 AGM season vote results for that pattern.

Toyota Industries shows the mechanism that does work reliably in Japan: not a shareholder vote to overrule management, but sustained public pressure on price during a going-private transaction the company had already agreed to pursue. Elliott didn't need 50% of the vote. It needed the deal's own economics — and enough public argument about fair value — to move the number before the tender closed. Two different levers, two different outcomes, in the same market in the same year. The vote is where Japanese activism hits a wall. The price is where it still moves.
